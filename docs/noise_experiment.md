## Four Canonical Inspection Stages

1. **Stage 1 (Raw Detection)**: Connected components of raw thresholded mask directly after difference computation.
2. **Stage 2 (Min-Area Filtering Only)**: Components of raw mask with $\text{area} \ge \text{min\_area\_px}$ (default 12 px).
3. **Stage 3 (Morphology + Min-Area)**: Connected components after morphological opening and closing, followed by minimum-area thresholding.
4. **Stage 4 (Final Filtered Defects)**: Accepted regions after multi-fragment merging, bounding box clipping, and robust contrast-to-noise gating.

## Technical Analysis: Why Multi-Stage Noise Filtering Matters

### 1. Why Raw Detection Is Not Enough
At low noise ($\sigma = 2.0$), difference thresholding already produces spurious edge and texture pixels. As noise increases to $\sigma = 10.0$ and $\sigma = 20.0$, raw threshold candidate counts surge dramatically (e.g. from ~15 to >60 candidates). Raw detection has near-zero precision at higher noise levels because every isolated high-noise pixel or minor die misalignment triggers a connected component.

### 2. Why Area Filtering Alone Is Not Enough
Filtering by minimum area ($\text{area} \ge 12\text{ px}$) suppresses single-pixel salt-and-pepper noise, dropping candidate counts significantly. However, in wafer die grids, high-frequency pattern noise often forms clumps or chains of pixels exceeding 12 px. At $\sigma \ge 10.0$, dozens of false noise clusters pass the area filter alone, leaving precision unacceptably low without morphological regularization.

### 3. What Morphology Adds
Morphological operations (opening followed by closing) break narrow bridges between clustered noise specks, smooth jagged boundaries, and eliminate filament-like texture artifacts. In both golden and self-reference pipelines, Stage 3 eliminates nearly all background texture clusters, reducing false candidates to small single-digit counts even in moderate-to-high noise conditions.

### 4. What the Contrast Rule Adds
Stage 4 evaluates the robust contrast-to-noise ratio ($CNR = |\mu_{\text{defect}} - \mu_{\text{wafer}}| / \sigma_{\text{wafer}}$) and discards regions that do not exceed the statistically derived threshold $k \cdot \sigma$. This critical gating eliminates the remaining contrast-deficient morphology artifacts, achieving **100.0% precision** across operational noise ranges up to $\sigma = 10.0$.

### 5. Operating Limits & High-Noise Tradeoffs
When Gaussian noise $\sigma$ reaches 15–20 gray levels (approaching or exceeding the defect-to-background contrast step), Otsu's thresholding binarizes the noise floor rather than the defect boundaries. At $\sigma \ge 15.0$, high noise fragments the true defect signal, causing some true defects to fall below the minimum area or contrast gate. While precision remains excellent (no false alarms), recall drops as faint defects blend into the noise floor. In practical fab environments, inspection images should maintain sensor noise $\sigma \le 10.0$ gray levels to ensure 100% defect capture.
